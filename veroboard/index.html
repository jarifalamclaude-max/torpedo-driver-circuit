Before you solder

  • Drawn for dot-type veroboard (one pad per hole, 2.54 mm); the board size for the chosen spacing is shown above the drawing. Every line is an insulated wire on the solder side, so wires may cross. On strip board you would have to cut the strips under every part first.
  • Isolation: columns 1–14 are the logic side (GNDL), columns 17–40 the power side (GNDP); columns 15–16 stay empty. Only the six optocouplers U2–U7 cross the gap. Never run a wire across it, and never join GNDL to GNDP.
  • Thick lines carry battery and solenoid current (about 1.1 A per 15 Ω solenoid). Use 0.5 mm² (20 AWG) or thicker for them. Thin lines are signals; 30 AWG wire-wrap or thin hookup wire is enough.
  • The XT60 board connector and the 5×20 fuse clips do not fit a 2.54 mm grid. Solder an XT60 pigtail at J1, and use an inline or two-leg 5×20 fuse holder at F2 with a 5 A fast ceramic fuse (F5AH).
  • TO-220 pins are G, D, S left to right with the printed face toward you. PC817 pin 1 is at the dot; U7 is turned round on purpose. The LP2950-3.3 (or 78L33) is OUT, GND, IN with the flat face toward you. Do not use an HT7333 or MCP1700 there: their pins are in a different order.
  • D1, D2 must be UF5408 or 1N5408, not a Schottky: Schottky leakage hides a disconnected solenoid from the self-test. Check every diode band against the K mark.
  • The divider resistors on the ADC inputs (R5, R6, R10, R11, R22–R25) and R12 should be 1 % metal film.
  • The ADS1115 module (U9) plugs into a 1×10 female header. Check its pin order (VDD GND SCL SDA ADDR ALRT A0 A1 A2 A3) before soldering the header.
